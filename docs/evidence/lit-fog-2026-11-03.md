# the lit ITEM family had no fog, and now it has one (2026-11-03)

The lit ITEM family is the pipeline every lit immediate batch goes through: entities, TESRs, the held item, dropped
items, and the per-group model path when the instanced one is off. Its push block spends 128..215 on the lighting
payload (two model-space light directions, the scene ambient, each light's diffuse, and the light-map coordinate pair at
208), and the shared fog block lives at 128..207 - the same bytes. The family therefore declared no fog at all, and
`celtium$writeFogPush` was called only for groups that carry no lighting. Every lit batch was drawn unfogged: a mob in
water, in the Nether or under the blindness effect stayed crisp while the terrain around it dissolved into the fog
colour. `plan.md` carried it as "次の標的 ②非instance lit fog".

This round gives the family its own fog block and measures the picture it produces.

## The change

* **The block.** `immediate_tex_color_lit.vert`/`.frag` declare `u_FogColor` at 224 and `u_FogParams` at 240, and the
  pipeline's range grows from 216 to 256. 216..223 stays free because the record-reading path stores its bindless
  texture index there (`ImmediateDrawRecords.record`), and 224/240 are the first 16-byte-aligned offsets past it - a
  `vec4` at 216 is a compile error (`'offset' : must be a multiple of the member's alignment`), which is why the block
  is 256 rather than 248.
* **The eye position.** The unlit families get the fog coordinate from the model-view rows at 160/176/192. The lit
  family's block carries the *combined* projection (P * MV) at 0 and no model view, so the vertex stage reconstructs
  the eye position from the perspective structure instead: `clip.x = P00 * eye.x`, `clip.y = P11 * eye.y`,
  `clip.w = -eye.z`. The two scales ride in the lighting payload's unused `.w` components (`u_Light0Dir.w`,
  `u_Light1Dir.w`), which the lighting eval never reads, so the reconstruction costs no extra bytes. A projection that
  is not a symmetric perspective (P02/P12/P03/P13 non-zero, or P11 != -1) is drawn unfogged and counted
  (`litFogSkippedNonPerspective`) rather than reconstructed approximately; the GUI's orthographic projection is that
  case and has fog off anyway.
* **The fragment stage.** The vertex stage hands it a flat `(u_FogColor, u_FogParams)` pair and the interpolated
  distance, so the fog curve is evaluated per fragment exactly where GL evaluates it - and the record-reading variant,
  whose push block is one per run rather than one per draw, reads its own record's fog instead of the run's.
* **The record stride.** 224 -> 256, with the unlit struct padded to the same stride and the lit struct gaining the fog
  fields. The lit record's own fog is what makes the fix work on the indirect path too.
* **The push-block cache.** It copied 216 bytes and did not key on the switch; it now copies 256 and mixes the switch
  into the key, so a cached block cannot carry the other arm's fog.
* **The device contract.** `VulkanContext.PUSH_CONSTANT_BYTES_USED` was 208 while the lit layout already declared 216:
  a device reporting 208..215 would have been selected and then failed inside `vkCreatePipelineLayout`. It now names
  256, the largest block any family declares, so a narrower device is skipped at selection with its own number.
* **The switch.** `-Dceltium.vulkan.litFog` (default true) and a runtime setter `celtium$setLitFog`, which the fixture
  uses for the within-run A/B below.

## The measurement

Leg `r43h-gate` (AMD Radeon RX 6400 / RADV NAVI24, `plain`, Supersymmetry, 1280x720, `guiScale3.txt`, validation ON,
renderer `d8a0689ac1c5a396da0f54bb21ea27a77f12006f52f73ff6a79635bea186a0c9`, fixture
`c98eb3addeb3ec226c06c7c3b652ee684d026757fe4a5b917485da6b5c852b61` - the same JARs the effect leg ran): **PASSED**, `accepted=true`, exit 0, `sceneCalibrationKeyMatches=true`, `sceneGateCalibrated=true`,
`presentedImageVerdict=PASSED`, `presentedDistinctColours=10652`, `presentedNonBackgroundFraction=0.930798`,
`presentedBlackFraction=0.00377`, 0 `VUID` lines, `CELTIUM_REFUSALS_BY_FORMAT {}`, `litFogGroups=986`,
`litFogSkippedNonPerspective=0`. The 986 are the flat world's chest and enchanting-table TESRs and the model draws that
take the per-group path; at the default fog end (~154 blocks) their weight is ~0.04, so the picture moves toward vanilla
by a few units and the gate's floors do not resolve it. The leg is the regression check, not the effect measurement.

### A cross-run pair cannot answer this question

Legs `r43b-fogoff` and `r43c-fogon` are the same scene and flags with only `-Dceltium.vulkan.litFog` apart
(`keepEntities` + `captureEntities` + `testFogEnd=32`). Their presented dumps differ on **82.46 % of the frame**
(mean |A-B| 14.10, p99 91): the animated water/lava/fire textures, the clouds and the entity animation phases are
wall-clock driven, so two runs are two different pictures whatever the flag does. `r43c` reports `litFogGroups=84`
against `r43b`'s 0, which is the counter evidence that the block is written, but the picture pair is not evidence for
anything. This is the trap `verification-traps.md` names, measured on this scene.

### The within-run pair

The fixture gained `-Dceltium.validation.litFogFrames=a,b,c`: at world frame `a` it dumps the frame with the fog off,
at `b` it flips the switch on and dumps, at `c` it flips it back off and dumps. The three frames share the scene, the
camera and (within a tick) the animation phase, so the only difference between `a` and `b` is the flag, and `a` vs `c`
is the animation-noise floor the pair has to be read against.

Two fixture defects had to be fixed first, both measured:

| defect | measurement | fix |
|---|---|---|
| the drawn camera was not the pinned pose | `r43d`: two consecutive frames of one run, nothing changed between them, differed on 11 % of the frame and the difference image was the terrain's block edges - a sub-pixel camera shift. The renderer draws `lastTickPos + (pos - lastTickPos) * partialTicks`, and the fixture set only the current values | `celtium$setPoseOn` pins `prevPos*`/`lastTickPos*` and `prevRotation*` with the pose, so the interpolation is the identity |
| the world scene is animated | `r43e` (pinned camera, default flat stack): `a` vs `c` still differed on 25 % of the frame - the enderman's portal particles, the animated water/lava/fire and the F3 overlay's own frame-time digits | the effect leg uses a static stack (`worldLayers=3;bedrock,2*dirt,grass;1;`) and reads the pair against the `a`/`c` noise mask |

Leg `r43g-fogmodels` (same GPU and instance, `plain`, static stack, `keepEntities` + `captureEntities`,
`testFogEnd=16` so the fog is LINEAR start=0 end=16, `-Dceltium.native.modelInstances=false` so the mobs and the chest
go through the per-group lit ITEM path this fix changes, `litFogFrames=60,61,62`, validation ON): `litFogGroups=1154`,
`litFogSkippedNonPerspective=0`, 0 `VUID` lines, `CELTIUM_REFUSALS_BY_FORMAT {}`. The verdict is
`UNCALIBRATED_PRESENTED_IMAGE` (exit 2) **by construction**: the entity variant and the controlled fog both move the
calibration key.

`python3 scripts/cpu-contract/lit-fog-check.py` on the three dumps, with the fog colour the leg's own
`CELTIUM_FOG_STATE` reports (`colour=0.70022243,0.8111807,0.99999976` = 179,207,255):

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| noise floor (`a` vs `c`, same arm, two frames apart) | 65 108 pixels changed (7.06 %), mean |A-B| 0.62 |
| pair (`a` vs `b`, fog off vs on) | 13 479 pixels changed (1.46 %), 5 555 excluded by the noise mask |
| pixels that fit `after = before + (fog - before) * w` | **10 355 (76.8 % of the changed pixels)** |
| solved weight min / p50 / p99 / max | **0.121 / 0.506 / 0.741 / 0.772** |
| model residual p50 / p99 over the fitting pixels | **0.29 / 1.14** (of 255) |
| sky band (top 108 rows) changed | **0** |

The weights are the distances: LINEAR fog with start 0 and end 16 gives `w = d / 16`, so 0.12..0.77 is 1.9..12.3
blocks - the frozen mobs at 8-12 blocks (0.50-0.75), the chest and sign at 6-7 (0.37-0.44) and the dropped item at 5
(0.31). The one-parameter model reproduces 10 355 pixels to a median of 0.29/255, which is what a fog mix does and what
a moved vertex, a changed colour or a reordered draw does not. The 3 124 changed pixels that do not fit are the hotbar
and the hand (blocks y 544-704, x 544-832): the GUI's own frame-to-frame content, where the fog is off in both arms.

The checker's own controls are in `scripts/tests/test_lit_fog_check.py` (7 tests): a synthetic mix at a known weight is
recovered and passes, a band painted a fixed colour fails, a change in the sky band fails, identical arms fail, an
overshooting mix fails, and the positive/negative control pair passes/refuses.

## What this does not claim

* The lit fog's **default** effect on the shipped scenes is small: the calibrated plain scene's lit geometry is within a
  block of the eye, and the entity scene's mobs go through the instanced path, which already had fog. The effect is
  measured where it is large - a controlled fog with the per-group path forced - not on a normal play session.
* The **fog colour and curve** are the shared block's, unchanged; this round moves them to the lit family, it does not
  re-derive them. The weight is checked against the distance ratio the leg's own fog state names, not against a
  Celeritas arm (no reference arm was run for this scene).
* **Intel** is not run this round. The device contract now requires `maxPushConstantsSize >= 256`; both measured GPUs
  report 256, and a narrower device is skipped at selection with its own number rather than failing later.
* The **record-reading path** (`-Dceltium.vulkan.indirectImmediate=true`) is not exercised by these legs; its stride and
  struct are held to the push block by `ImmediateDrawRecordAbiTest`, which is a layout test, not a picture.
* No FPS claim: the fog adds one integer test per vertex when the selector is 0 and the reconstruction only when it is
  not; no frame-time measurement was taken.

  --fog 179,207,255
